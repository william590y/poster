About 1.25 minutes. Task: [0, x1, x2, (x1 + x2) mod 256], x1, x2 != 0 (App. D); 1,024 trials; one learner, scale not stated. Entropy: of the next-byte distribution at the answer position, mean and IQR over trials (the position is my inference; the caption says "predictive entropy"). Stages (percentages, m ranges and entropy values are plot reads): m = 0: "preferred bytes" {0, 255, 1, 16}, "the marginally most common bytes" (p.11), ~92% of argmax; entropy ~7.6 bits. The set {0, 255, 1, 16} is printed on the figure's dark-grey band. After a few examples: copies context bytes, then "loses confidence"; entropy ~7.8 bits near m = 4-8 (uniform = 8; the dashed line in the right panel). "After around 4 examples" it sums the low 4 bits; "by 8 examples" the high 4 bits too (p.11). m = 512: ~87% fully correct, but mean entropy still ~5 bits. Fig. 5, p.11. Paper's narrative (p.11): the prior, then copying previous bytes, then it 'loses confidence after several overconfident but incorrect predictions, reverting to a very broad distribution', then low bits after ~4 examples, high bits by 8, then it 'locks in on the correct approach'. Plot reads (left panel): m = 0 preferred ~92%, copy-context ~8%; m = 64 ~56% fully correct, with ~88% having the low 4 bits right; m = 512 ~87%. Right panel: ~6.9 bits at m = 64, ~5.05 at m = 512. [mine] Argmax is right by m = 512, but the distribution is still diffuse on a deterministic task; the paper does not discuss this. Speculative: 16 is a preferred byte, and macro X sets a cell to 16, so the prior may reflect the language, not natural data. The copy phase may depend on the prompt's byte format, which is untested.
