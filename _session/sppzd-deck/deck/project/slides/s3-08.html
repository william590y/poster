About 1.5 minutes. Setup: a 24.4M model, random init vs the final self-play checkpoint, on DCLM, CIFAR-10 and ESC-50 (App. A.1, p.21). Natural-data tokens consumed to convergence, warm vs scratch (p.21): ESC-50 320M vs 496M (-35%, my calc); CIFAR-10 421M vs 588M (-28%, my calc); DCLM has no count in the text, Fig. 6 only. Corpora ~255M / 146M / 152M tokens (DCLM / CIFAR-10 / ESC-50), repeated over epochs (A.2). Each arm tuned separately (LR x WD grid, 4 seeds per configuration); convergence = < 0.005 improvement for 5 evaluations, then a 200-step cosine decay (A.2). Gap "narrowed considerably" by the end (p.21); self-play compute not counted, "a one-time cost that amortizes" (p.22). Figs. and text pp.21-22. Grids (A.2): LR in {1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2}, WD in {0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.8}. Per arm, the configuration with the lowest mean converged BPB is kept. Caption: 'the warm start reaches every loss level first'. Open markers show the mean tokens consumed at convergence. Plot reads at 0 tokens (zero-shot): DCLM ~5.7 vs ~8.6, CIFAR-10 ~6.3 vs ~8.7, ESC-50 ~5.3 vs ~8.6. DCLM convergence markers read ~470M vs ~530M. Converged BPB is about 1.5-1.6 on DCLM and ~3.6-3.9 on CIFAR-10 and ESC-50 for both arms. The term 'pre-pretraining' is from Hu et al. 2025 (cited in A.1). [mine] One scale, and the claim is speed, not final loss. The 0-token DCLM warm start (~5.7) sits well above the Fig. 2 DCLM endpoint (~4.4). The paper does not reconcile them; possible reasons are a single model vs ensemble, checkpoint choice, or validation split. Do not put both on screen without saying so.
