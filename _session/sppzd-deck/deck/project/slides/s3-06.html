About 1.75 minutes. Format: m examples of [0, x1..xk, f(x)], then a query; greedy argmax exact match; no gradient updates (p.10; App. D, Eq. 14). Chance: 1/256 ~ 0.004 for a uniform byte guess (my calc). The y-axis is nonlinear. There is no dashed chance line drawn on the plot (an overlay would not line up), so say the chance level out loud: about 0.004. Self-play: reverse string, stack and associative recall reach "almost 100%" after a sufficient number of examples (p.11). MAX and MIN at m = 0: the text reports 6%-8% from a copying prior (p.11); the plot reads ~4-5%. SUM at m = 0 is at chance. Universal prior: "little evidence of effective ICL"; SUM at most ~1.5% (Fig. 4 caption; plot read). PCFG: "performs strongly on associative recall but transfers only weakly to the remaining tasks" (Fig. 4 caption). Not stated: model scale (only "below 25M", p.15), dictionary size V, k, instance counts. Suggested walk-through order (all three panels are on screen at once): (1) the Self-play panel, pointing at reverse string, stack and assoc; (2) the Universal prior and PCFG panels; (3) the PCFG assoc curve. Fig. 4, p.10; the same tasks appear in Fig. 1 (lower right). Plot reads at the largest m: stack and reverse ~1.0, assoc ~0.9-1.0, SUM ~0.85, MAX and MIN ~0.65-0.7. Reverse and stack stop near m ~ 128. Also at m = 0 (plot reads, not discussed in the paper): self-play stack ~0.35; PCFG assoc ~0.6 and PCFG MAX/MIN ~0.35, which then collapse. Prepare for questions on these. p.9 says PCFG and universal-prior models 'fail on our ICL evaluations', while the Fig. 4 caption credits PCFG on assoc; quote the caption. Tasks (App. D): SUM = (x1+x2) mod 256 with x1, x2 != 0; STACK push = 250, pop = 251; ASSOC dictionary over bytes 1-255. [mine] The jagged PCFG curves suggest few instances. The tasks are close to the machine's own operations, so 'completely held-out tasks' (p.2) is plausible but untested.
