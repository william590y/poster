Method

Reward: alignment with the learner's own progress

Reward = how far a program's gradient points along the learner's preconditioned parameter movement over the lookback window

δθ_e: learner movement over a lookback window that grows with training

P_e: the diagonal AdamW step operator

Paper's Eq. 2, p.5

Expected (p.5): little reward for mastered programs (near-zero gradients) and for unrelated or unlearnable ones (no alignment)

Our reading: to first order, roughly the loss decrease on y_i between θ_⌊e/2⌋ and θ_e, unsigned and preconditioned

Lead: reward = how far a program's gradient points along the learner's preconditioned parameter movement over the lookback window. r_i = | ⟨grad_θ L(y_i; θ_e), P_e ⊙ δθ_e⟩ | (Eq. 2, p.5); δθ_e = θ_{floor(e/2)} - θ_e; P_e = lr / (sqrt(v_hat_e) + eps), the diagonal AdamW step operator. Expected (p.5): mastered programs give near-zero gradients; unrelated or unlearnable ones do not align with the movement; both get little reward. First order (my reading): roughly the loss decrease on y_i between θ_{floor(e/2)} and θ_e, unsigned and preconditioned. Forward-mode autodiff, no full gradients (p.6); one JVP per program is my reading. Lookback grows with training; reward chosen "after searching through several possibilities at small scale" (p.5). What each piece buys: Table 5, in Results. Detail: Eq. 2 is on p.5; typeset crop at figs/eq-generator-reward.png. Window: the text's "lookback window of ceil(e/2)" is the length e - floor(e/2), so it agrees with Eq. 2's checkpoint θ_{floor(e/2)} and with Table 5's caption. The window "helps average over short-term fluctuations" and "allows early mistakes to eventually be forgotten" (p.5).
